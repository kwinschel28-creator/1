# Moat Analysis

What this note is for: how you judge whether a competitive advantage is real, where it comes from, and whether it is widening or eroding. The agent scores the moat criterion against this.

## Default framework (replace with your own)

Judge a moat by **source → durability → evidence it is real**.

Sources:
- **Switching costs**: leaving is costly, risky, or a hassle. Evidence: retention, net revenue retention, contract length, depth of integration.
- **Network effects**: value rises with users; winner-take-most. Evidence: share, cross-side growth, engagement.
- **Scale economies**: durably lower unit cost rivals can't match. Evidence: cost-per-unit vs peers, fixed-cost leverage.
- **Intangibles**: brand that commands price, patents, licenses. Evidence: realized pricing power, gross-margin premium, protection runway.
- **Regulatory / structural**: licenses, approvals, entrenched standards. Evidence: barrier height and durability.
- **Cost advantage**: privileged inputs, location, process.

Durability test: would the advantage survive a well-funded, competent attacker for 10 years? Name exactly what erodes it and whether that erosion is visible yet. A moat you can't see in the numbers (pricing power, ROIC above cost of capital sustained over years, share stability) is a story, not a moat.
