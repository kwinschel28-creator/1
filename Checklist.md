### Hard disqualifiers (one trip = Fail and stop, fast)

- **Accounting smell.** Cumulative free cash flow well below cumulative net income across 3+ years with no clean explanation; receivables or inventory growing materially faster than revenue; repeated "one-time" charges; restatements; auditor or CFO turnover; going-concern language. One credible flag is enough to stop.
- **Balance-sheet fragility.** Near-term maturities it cannot plausibly refinance at a survivable rate; covenant headroom thin; net debt/EBITDA above your ceiling (default >3.5x for non-defensive, >2x for cyclicals); negative tangible equity while free cash flow is negative.
- **Governance and dilution.** Controlling holder extracting value; related-party deals against minorities; share count rising materially year over year with no commensurate per-share value created; pay untethered from per-share results; promotional management that sells the stock more than it runs the business.
- **Unexplainable business.** (Sector exclusions removed 2026-07-16, see amendment log in [[Philosophy]].) Any sector is eligible, but if I cannot answer the three-question check below on a specific company, it is out regardless of sector. Balance-sheet-opaque models (banks, insurers, REITs, utilities) require the agent's essential-information brief plus the agent's full written deep dive before any buy. Per the 2026-08-10 amendment the research is the agent's to produce; the three-question check is mine, and it is the gate.

Examples to keep or replace: serial acquirers that never show organic growth; "TAM times a percent" decks with no unit economics; companies whose IR answers questions with adjusted metrics only; heavy reliance on a single customer or regulator.

### Lane A checklist: quality compounders (each must pass before you size)

- **Business and unit economics.** I can state the revenue drivers, the cost structure, and what one incremental unit earns.
- **Moat, evidenced not asserted.** Name the specific advantage, the proof it exists (pricing, retention, share, returns on capital through a cycle), and whether it is widening or narrowing.
- **Returns on capital.** ROIC/ROCE above the cost of capital and stable-to-rising, with a credible runway to reinvest at those rates.
- **Management and capital allocation.** Honest, candid in bad quarters, and a track record of sensible reinvestment, buybacks only when cheap, and disciplined M&A. Check what they are actually paid for: outcomes (ROIC, free cash flow, per-share value) versus activity (size, revenue growth, headcount). Activity-based pay predicts empire-building; price it in.
- **Survivability.** The balance sheet and cash generation get through a bad year without a dilutive raise.
- **Valuation and margin of safety.** Run the price backwards (reverse DCF or implied growth): what must be true to justify today's price, and is that conservative or heroic? Buy only with a gap between price and a conservative value.
- **Three falsifiers.** The three things that would make me wrong, written down in advance, each one monitorable.
- **Why does this exist.** What do I see that the marginal seller does not, and why is the mispricing available to me?

### Lane A quantitative gates (suggested defaults, tune them) [YOURS to finalize]

- Min ROIC/ROCE: >15% sustained; >20% to earn the "quality compounder" label.
- Max leverage: net debt/EBITDA <3.5x (lower for cyclicals); interest coverage (EBIT/interest) >5x.
- Liquidity: no maturity inside 18 to 24 months it cannot cover from cash plus free cash flow.
- Valuation ceiling: do not initiate above the implied growth you actually believe; per sector, set a FCF-yield floor or multiple cap so "great business" never becomes a license to overpay.
- Margin of safety: for value names, price below roughly 70% of a conservative intrinsic value estimate; for compounders, a defensible entry multiple versus history and growth.

### The question I always forget to ask (force this) [YOURS, keep or replace]

- "What has to be true in the numbers in three years for this to work, and is that probable or merely possible?"
- "Who is selling to me at this price, and what do they know or believe that I am dismissing?"

### Lane B gates: insider / special-situations (different bar)

For ideas sourced from the insider screen below, do NOT apply the durable-compounder quality bar. Judge instead on:
- **Fundamental inflection.** Is the operating thesis actually playing out: are earnings, margins, or cash flow improving, not just promised? Evidence, not narrative.
- **Balance-sheet repair and survivability.** Can it fund the turn without a dilutive raise? Are near-term maturities covered? This is make-or-break for a turnaround.
- **Catalyst path.** A concrete, datable path (cost-out, asset sale, new-management actions, activist milestones), not "eventually."
- **Survivable downside.** If the turn fails, what is the loss? Size for asymmetry; the setup must pay me several times the downside for the risk it does not work.
- **Hard kills still apply** (accounting smell, unfundable balance sheet, governance, out-of-circle, can't-model).

Valuation in Lane B: pay for the turn at a price where the downside is survivable and the upside is a multiple of it. Compounder multiple discipline does not apply here; asymmetry does.

### Insider-buying signal screen (yours, from your notes)

Premise: real alignment shows up in what insiders do with their own money, not in what they say. Managers paid for activity (size, revenue growth) behave differently from those paid for outcomes (ROIC, free cash flow, per-share value). Use this to source ideas, not to buy on the signal alone.

A buy counts as signal only if:
- It is a true open-market purchase (Form 4 code P), not an option exercise or vesting.
- The dollar amount is material relative to that person's compensation. A $100K buy from a director on $500K comp outweighs $100K from an exec on $20M.
- Multiple insiders buy within 4 to 6 weeks of each other (clustering), not a lone buyer.
- It happens with the stock down or the company in transition: spinoff, new CEO, activist pressure, strategic review.
- It reverses prior selling. A buy from someone who had been trimming is a signal flip.

Before building a thesis, force these questions:
- Is the balance sheet actually stronger than the market thinks, or is the insider just optimistic?
- Is the new CEO actually cutting costs, or only talking about it?
- Has the noncore asset review actually begun, or is it merely planned?
- Is the stock genuinely down on temporary problems, or is there a structural issue?

Workflow (do not skip 3 and 4):
1. Signal.
2. Screen Form 4s.
3. Find clustering.
4. Build an operating thesis.
5. Check whether earnings will actually improve.
6. Buy only if valuation is reasonable.

Most people stop at the signal and wonder why insider buying "didn't work." The buying is the lookup; steps 4 and 5 are the edge.

Failure modes to enforce on me (added):
- Discount 10b5-1 and other automatic or scheduled buys; they are not discretionary conviction.
- Insiders are early and often wrong on timing. They know the business, not the multiple or the macro. A cluster is a reason to research, never a license to skip valuation.
- Mind the base rate and my own memory. I will recall the insider-cluster winners and forget the ones that kept falling. The operating thesis, not the Form 4, has to carry the position.
