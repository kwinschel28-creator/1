## How I invest: two lanes, one discipline

I run two lanes with different bars. The hard disqualifiers apply to both. The quality bar does not.

### Lane A: quality compounders (core)
- High and durable returns on capital (ROIC/ROCE comfortably above cost of capital, stable-to-rising), a long runway to reinvest at those rates, real pricing power, and cash conversion that shows up as free cash flow rather than getting eaten by capex and working capital.
- Bought on dislocation, not at any price: own them when a temporary, fixable problem or a market-wide drawdown puts them at a multiple I can defend, not when the narrative is loudest.
- Judged on the Lane A gates in the Checklist (durable quality plus valuation).

### Lane B: insider / special-situations
- Sourced from clustered, material, discretionary insider buying in transition situations (see the insider screen in the Checklist).
- Judged on a different bar: fundamental inflection (are earnings actually improving, not just promised), balance-sheet repair and survivability (can it fund the turn without a dilutive raise), a concrete catalyst path, and a survivable downside if the turn fails. NOT durable-compounder quality.
- The edge is the operating thesis, the work most people skip (steps 3 and 4 of the insider workflow), not the Form 4 itself.

### No momentum sleeve (decided)
- I do not run a momentum or technical book. Any chart-driven idea must still clear Lane A or Lane B gates. This is the firewall that stops a trade from quietly becoming a "long-term hold" after it goes against me.

## My real edge (honest, no flattery)
- Primary, behavioral: a holding period and tolerance for volatility most of the market does not have. Sitting still and not selling quality into fear is the hardest edge to arbitrage away.
- Sourcing and process (yours): I find ideas from clustered, material insider buying in transition situations, then do the operating-thesis work most people skip. The Form 4 gets me to look; the edge lives in whether earnings actually improve.
- How I read management (yours): I weight managers paid for outcomes (ROIC, free cash flow, per-share value) over those paid for activity (size, revenue growth, headcount). Comp structure tells me which behavior I am underwriting before I read a single strategy slide.
- What I do NOT have: an informational edge in liquid large caps. Where my only "edge" is having read a bull thread, I have no edge. Cloning is not a thesis.

## Time horizon and target return
- Horizon: 3 to 5 years minimum per core position. I am buying the business, not renting the ticker.
- Target return and benchmark: **[YOURS]** set a real number versus a benchmark. Default discipline: beat a broad index after costs and tax over rolling 5-year windows. If I cannot say why an idea beats simply owning the index, the index is the position.

## What I refuse to own no matter how cheap
- Anything I cannot explain in plain terms, or whose edge I cannot say why it lasts.
- Accounting or governance smells (see Checklist hard disqualifiers); cheapness does not fix dishonesty.
- Balance sheets that cannot survive a bad year.
- Pure-narrative stocks where the entire thesis is story plus price action with no credible path to cash generation.
- Anything bought mainly because an account I follow owns it.
