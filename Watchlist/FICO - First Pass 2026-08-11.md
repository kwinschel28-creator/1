# FICO (Fair Isaac) - First Pass 2026-08-11

Anchor: 1,049.74 (2026-08-10 close). Down 47.5% from its 12-month high, roughly half from the 2,206 high set May 2025. Street target 1,489.

## Verdict: CONCERN. Tag this BREAK, not SENT

The screen ranked FICO first on quality: ROIC 61.9% against 51.3% a year ago, operating margin 53.8% against 48.9%, revenue +25.7%, Piotroski 8. Every one of those is a **trailing** number, and there is a dated event between them and today.

**The monopoly took a direct regulatory hit and responded with an 80% price cut.**

- FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 alongside FICO 10T for Fannie and Freddie.
- **April 23, 2026:** Freddie Mac began accepting VantageScore for mortgage underwriting, Fannie followed.
- VantageScore is priced as low as ~$1 per score against roughly $10 for a FICO score.
- **April 2026:** FICO cut its FICO 10T mortgage score from $4.95 to $0.99, matching the competitor.

That is not sentiment. It is the pricing power of the mortgage segment being reset in public, by regulators, with the incumbent conceding on price. The 47.5% drawdown is the market repricing a monopoly into a duopoly.

The trailing metrics the screen liked mostly predate the cut taking effect. Buying on ROIC 61.9% is buying the old pricing.

## The balance sheet is not the fortress the ratios imply

- **Debt jumped from 3,657M to 5,598M in a single quarter**, roughly +$1.94B, and from 2,231M two years ago. Debt has more than doubled.
- **Book equity is negative** (D/E returns null), the result of years of debt-funded buybacks. Shares 24.5M to 23.2M.
- Current ratio 1.18 and has swung between 0.83 and 2.22, so liquidity is managed tightly.
- SBC is 7.8% of revenue, high in absolute terms though normal for software.
- Short interest 12.5% of float.

A levered equity with negative book value, going through a price war in its most profitable segment, is a different risk profile from "62% ROIC compounder."

## What is genuinely intact

The scores segment is only part of the business; the software and platform segment is growing and is not exposed to the mortgage-score fight. B2B non-mortgage scoring is unaffected by the FHFA decision. Revenue accelerated to +25.7% and margins expanded through the quarter the cut landed, so the near-term damage has not shown up yet. Forward P/E 24.4 on next-year EPS of 53.13 is not demanding for the franchise that remains.

## Insider file

Twelve months, open-market, non-10b5-1: **0 buyers, 4 sellers, ~$7.66M sold.** Last sale 2026-02-13, before the April price cut. No insider has bought into a 47% decline.

## What would change the verdict

1. One or two quarters showing mortgage-score revenue holding despite the $0.99 price, which would mean volume offset price.
2. Any insider purchase at these levels.
3. A view Kevin can state on whether credit-score pricing power survives a regulator-sponsored competitor. If he cannot state it, [[Philosophy]] says size is the discipline, not the thesis.

This is a real candidate, not a reject. But it is a **contested thesis on a levered balance sheet**, not the clean quality-dislocation the screen made it look like, and it should not be bought on the screen's summary line.
