# Candidate Shortlist, 2026-06-30

> These are research starting points, not picks, and not advice. All four are Lane A quality-compounder candidates in or near your circle (SaaS, healthcare, biopharma). None come from your actual edge, which is insider-cluster special situations; that needs a separate Form 4 screen (see end). Each entry leads with the bear case on purpose. Rule: if you cannot write one honest sentence of thesis in your own words, it is a pass. Prices are approximate, as of late June 2026, and sources sometimes disagree; treat exact multiples as Unknown until you confirm.

## VEEV, Veeva Systems (Lane A; squarely in circle: SaaS + pharma)
- **What it is:** the dominant vertical-SaaS system of record for the life-sciences industry (CRM, clinical, quality, regulatory). Sticky, high-margin, founder-led.
- **Bear case first:** Citi cut its target from $291 to $176 in April 2026 on a billings-growth slowdown and a shift toward lower-margin services. The open question is whether the deceleration is cyclical (pharma budget tightening) or structural (saturation, or AI eroding seat-based software). If structural, the compounding thesis breaks.
- **Bull case:** near-monopoly in a regulated vertical with brutal switching costs; ~16% revenue growth to $3.2B in FY2026; the stock is down about 24% over the past year, so for once a quality name is dislocated, which is your stated entry style.
- **Valuation:** roughly $176, forward P/E high-teens to ~20x. Not cheap, not demanding for the quality.
- **First-pass verdict: Dig deeper.** Best circle fit on this list and actually dislocated.
- **Your call:** is the billings slowdown temporary? If you believe pharma IT spend recovers and seats hold against AI, that is a one-sentence thesis. If you can't say why, pass.

## VRTX, Vertex Pharmaceuticals (Lane A; in circle: commercial biopharma)
- **What it is:** the monopoly in cystic fibrosis, now adding real new legs: Casgevy (gene therapy for sickle cell and beta-thalassemia) and Journavx (the first new-mechanism non-opioid acute pain drug, 350k scripts in Q1, 240M lives covered).
- **Bear case first:** still heavily concentrated in CF; the new drivers must scale to matter, and gene therapy and a novel pain launch both carry reimbursement and adoption risk. Long term, CF patent and competitive exposure. This is commercial-stage, not clinical, so it fits your circle, but pipeline binaries still lurk.
- **Bull case:** durable, highly profitable franchise (~$13B 2026 revenue guide), pricing power, and Journavx is a genuinely large non-opioid pain opportunity if the ramp holds.
- **Valuation:** roughly $445, about 12% off its high, earnings multiple in the mid-20s (approximate, confirm).
- **First-pass verdict: Dig deeper.** Real franchise plus a real catalyst, in your circle.
- **Your call:** do you believe Journavx becomes a multi-billion drug? That is the swing factor and it is checkable in script trends each quarter.

## IDXX, IDEXX Laboratories (Lane A; adjacent: animal-health diagnostics, not human)
- **What it is:** razor-and-blade leader in companion-animal diagnostics; recurring instrument-and-consumable model with very high returns on capital.
- **Bear case first:** expensive at roughly 38x earnings (~$572, ~$15 2026 EPS), and growth has stepped down from mid-teens to high-single-digits (2026 organic 7.7 to 9.7%) on soft vet-visit volumes. You would be paying a compounder multiple for decelerating growth, which is exactly the trap your valuation discipline exists to catch.
- **Bull case:** elite quality, durable moat, recurring revenue, and a long runway if pet-care spend reaccelerates.
- **Valuation:** rich. This is a price-discipline call, not a quality call.
- **First-pass verdict: Watchlist.** Quality yes, entry no, and it is adjacent to rather than inside your circle (animal, not human, health).
- **Your call:** what multiple would you pay? Set a price, wait.

## GWRE, Guidewire (Lane A; in circle: SaaS)
- **What it is:** the dominant core-systems platform for P&C insurers, well into a successful cloud transition; ARR growing ~19 to 22%, subscription revenue +35%.
- **Bear case first:** valuation is steep (TTM P/E around 70) and profitability is still thin, so the stock is volatile; it dropped from the $160s to about $121 in late June 2026 on an earnings reaction despite raising guidance. You are paying up for a transition that is mostly priced in.
- **Bull case:** entrenched franchise, cloud migration with years to run, expanding margins as cloud scales. The recent selloff could be the dislocation you look for.
- **Valuation:** roughly $121 to $160 depending on the day; expensive on earnings, more reasonable on forward ARR.
- **First-pass verdict: Watchlist.** Great franchise, demanding price; the selloff is worth understanding before acting.
- **Your call:** is the late-June drop a thesis problem or a multiple problem? If only the multiple moved, that is your entry question.
