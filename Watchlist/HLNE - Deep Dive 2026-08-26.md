# HLNE (Hamilton Lane) - Deep Dive 2026-08-26

Anchor: 106.83 (2026-08-25 close). Market cap $5.93B, equity $939.3M, P/B 6.32. Fiscal year ends March, so the June 2026 quarter is Q1 FY27. Street target 133.57. Short interest 11.0% of float. Insider ownership 11.6%.

## Verdict: WATCHLIST maintained. It cannot reach PASS on current evidence

The 8/18 ledger row held HLNE at watchlist and said it was held "NOT on the numbers," blocked only on the Philosophy elevated-risk regime because Hamilton Lane is an opaque-model financial. **That framing was wrong, and this is the correction: the elevated-risk gate was never the binding constraint.** HLNE fails the PASS condition itself, on three independent counts, and the deep dive that was supposedly owed does not change any of them.

1. **The price went UP on the print.** The PASS condition is the GWRE configuration: results delivered, guidance raised or reaffirmed healthy, **while the price fell**. HLNE rose 5.18% to $99.83 on the 2026-08-04 Q1 FY27 release. It is not a results-up/price-down overreaction. It is a name the market re-rated in the direction of the news, which is the ordinary case and carries no edge.
2. **The revenue growth that cleared gate 4 is carried interest, not demand.** The 56.5% was driven by a 170% surge in incentive fees off a down comparison quarter.
3. **You would be paying 37% above the insiders.** The cluster that made this "arguably the best raw file of the entire sweep" bought at $76.27 to $78.73 on 2026-06-11. The stock is 106.83.

None of these needed a deep dive to find. Two needed the price checked against the transaction dates, and one needed the revenue line decomposed. Both are cheap. That is the process point at the bottom of this note.

## Item 1: the elevated-risk brief, delivered because it was owed

Philosophy requires the essential-information brief before any buy on an opaque model, regardless of verdict, so here it is even though the verdict does not turn on it.

**How it makes money.** Hamilton Lane is not an asset manager in the usual sense. It sits between institutional and wealth clients and private-markets funds, and it earns in three distinct ways with three different qualities:

- **Management and advisory fees** on discretionary assets, $146.3B of the $1.1T total. This is the durable, recurring, high-quality revenue and it is the only line that behaves like a subscription.
- **Advisory fees** on non-discretionary assets, $914.1B. This is the bulk of the headline AUM number and it is the low-fee-rate portion. **The $1.1 trillion figure is close to meaningless for valuation**, because the fee rate on the non-discretionary $914B is a small fraction of the rate on the discretionary $146B. Anyone quoting "$1.1T AUM" against a $5.9B market cap is comparing the wrong numerator.
- **Incentive fees, that is carried interest.** Lumpy, mark-driven, realised on fund exits. This is what moved the quarter.

**What the balance sheet hides.** Three things. (a) HLNE runs an **Up-C structure**, the same structure that makes RYAN's book leverage unreadable in this book already. GAAP net income attributable to Hamilton Lane Incorporated is a slice of total economics, so headline EPS and P/E understate the full entity and are not comparable to a normal C-corp. (b) The firm holds **balance-sheet investments in its own funds**, so equity carries mark-to-market exposure to the same private assets it charges fees on. When private marks compress, fees and the balance sheet fall together. That is a correlated, not diversified, risk. (c) **Accrued carried interest is an asset that can be clawed back.** Unrealised carry sits on the balance sheet and reverses if fund performance deteriorates before exit.

**The one or two numbers that break it.** Fee-earning AUM growth stalling, and private-market exit activity freezing. The second breaks incentive fees directly and the first breaks the recurring line with a lag. A prolonged shutdown in private-market exits does both at once, and it also compresses the marks on the balance-sheet investments. That is the single scenario that hits all three revenue lines and the balance sheet simultaneously.

**My reading: the regime applies and the business is knowable.** This is a fee-and-carry model, not a levered balance sheet. It is materially easier to understand than a bank or an insurer, and easier than PGY. If Kevin ever wanted to own a private-markets manager, this is a defensible one to own. The gate is satisfiable. It is simply not the reason to decline today.

## Item 2: decomposing the 56.5%. This is what breaks gate 4

Gate 4 requires real demand growth of at least 4% year over year, and it carries the WING guard: for models where reported revenue is not demand, check the organic line by hand. **An asset manager is exactly such a model, and the guard was not applied.**

Quarterly revenue, $M, and the year-over-year growth the gate reads:

| Quarter ending | Revenue | YoY growth |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-30 (Q1 FY27) | 275.3 | **+56.5%** |
| 2026-03-31 | 193.6 | -2.2% |
| 2025-12-31 | 198.6 | +18.0% |
| 2025-09-30 | 190.9 | +27.3% |
| 2025-06-30 (Q1 FY26) | 176.0 | **-10.6%** |
| 2025-03-31 | 198.0 | +12.1% |
| 2024-12-31 | 168.3 | +34.3% |
| 2024-09-30 | 150.0 | +18.2% |
| 2024-06-30 (Q1 FY25) | 196.7 | +57.3% |

Read the June quarters down the left: **196.7, then 176.0, then 275.3.** The +56.5% is measured against a quarter that was itself down 10.6%. Over two years the same June quarter compounds at **18.3% a year**, and TTM revenue growth is **+24.0%** ($858.4M against $692.3M). Both are good numbers. Neither is 56.5%.

The composition matters more than the rate. The company attributes the quarter to a **170% surge in incentive fees**, with management and advisory fees growing "steadily." Incentive fees are carried interest: realised on exits, lumpy by construction, and not a demand signal. The swing from -10.6% to +56.5% in four quarters is the signature of a carry line, not of a customer base.

**This does not make HLNE a bad business.** A 24% TTM growth rate with 45.9% operating margins is genuinely strong. It makes the gate-4 input wrong, and it means the growth leg of the quality case rests on exit markets staying open.

## Item 3: ROIC clears the band and fails the trajectory

Gate 1 passes: ROIC 22.4% now against 22.5% a year ago, 99.6% retention, comfortably inside the 90% band. That band was written for exactly this kind of one-decimal wobble and HLNE was the name that motivated it.

But the eight-quarter shape is the LII test, and the standing durability check is "ROIC trajectory not decaying, the LII/AZO lesson":

`25.8 → 26.6 → 24.6 → 22.5 → 21.7 → 20.4 → 19.9 → 22.4`

Peak to trough is 26.6 down to 19.9, a 25% decline, and the 22.4 that clears the band is a partial recovery driven by the same incentive-fee quarter that inflated revenue. LII was killed on 8/11 for "ROIC decaying monotonically 36.4% to 25.8% over 8 quarters." HLNE's decay is not monotonic and it is shallower, so this is not a symmetric kill. **But the gate as written compares two points 12 months apart and is blind to the six quarters in between.** Recorded as a gate-design observation, not as a HLNE-specific charge.

Piotroski tells the same story: 6 today, but 6, 6, 6, 4, 5, 4, 4, 5, 6 across the run. It oscillates with the carry line. [[Sourcing Rules]] already says Piotroski is a floor and never a ranker; on a carry-driven model it is barely even a floor.

## Item 4: what is genuinely good, stated plainly

The bear case above should not obscure a real business.

- **Operating margin 45.9%**, and above 41% in every one of the last ten quarters. This is a capital-light fee model and the margins are real.
- **Balance sheet is fine and improving.** Debt $364.8M against equity $939.3M, D/E 0.39 from 0.52 a year ago. No refinancing question.
- **Cash conversion is high.** TTM FCF $366.7M on TTM revenue $858.4M, a 42.7% FCF margin. TTM SBC of roughly $51M is 13.9% of FCF, which is not a dilution problem.
- **Structural tailwind is real.** The retail and wealth channel opening to private markets is a genuine multi-year demand driver and Hamilton Lane is a credible way to own it.
- **Dividend raised 11%** to a $2.40 target for the year, a 1.52% yield, which is a management signal about the durability of the fee base rather than of the carry.

## Item 6: valuation, and why it is not the deciding issue

Trailing P/E 21.5, forward P/E 18.6, EV/EBITDA 15.2, P/B 6.32, FCF yield 6.2%, street target 133.57 (+25%).

For a 45% operating-margin fee business growing TTM revenue 24%, an 18.6x forward multiple is not demanding. **Valuation is not why this is declined.** But two adjustments matter: the Up-C structure makes the P/E not directly comparable to a C-corp, and forward EPS estimates for a carry-driven model embed an assumption about exit markets that the multiple hides. A 6.2% FCF yield on a business where 40% of the growth is carry is less safe than the same yield on a subscription business.

## What would change the verdict

Any one of these, with a date attached:

1. **A real results-up/price-down event.** A future print where fee-earning AUM and management-fee revenue grow, the outlook holds, and the stock falls. That is the PASS condition and it has not occurred.
2. **Price back toward where insiders bought.** Below roughly $85, the June cluster stops being a 37% headwind and starts being support. That is a mechanical line, not a forecast.
3. **A fresh post-print cluster.** The 8/21 recency gate is satisfied by any purchase after 2026-08-04. There has been none. If Rogers or the co-CEOs buy again above 100, that is new information and it reverses the Item 5 objection directly.
4. **Management-fee-only growth disclosed above 10%** with incentive fees stripped out. That would repair gate 4 on its own terms.

**Next dated catalyst: Q2 FY27, expected early November 2026** (Q1 was 2026-08-04, fiscal year ends March). Roughly ten weeks out. Nothing about this name requires a decision before then.

## Process findings, which are the durable output here

1. **A name can be parked behind the wrong gate, and parking hides that.** The 8/18 row said HLNE was held only on the elevated-risk regime. Once examined, it fails the PASS condition three separate ways and the elevated-risk gate is the one thing it would have survived. A parked name should carry the reason it is parked AND the checks that were not run, or the next reader inherits a false picture of how close it was.
2. **Gate 8 measures the wrong distance.** Drawdown from a trailing high says nothing about whether the price is dislocated now. HLNE is 31.6% off its high and 39% above its low. Add a distance-from-insider-cost or distance-from-12-month-low check. See the systemic note in the 2026-08-26 re-underwrite for the third instance of this in nine days.
3. **The WING guard needs a named list of model types, not a case-by-case judgement.** It caught franchisors because WING forced it. It did not catch asset managers. Any model where reported revenue contains a performance-linked or transaction-linked component (asset managers, brokers, investment banks, royalty models, anything with carry or incentive fees) needs the organic line checked by hand.
