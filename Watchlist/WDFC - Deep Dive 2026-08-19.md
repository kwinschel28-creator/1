# WDFC (WD-40) - Deep Dive 2026-08-19

Anchor: 230.01 (2026-08-18 close). Market cap $3.09B, EV $3.15B, 13.46M shares. Fiscal year ends 8/31.

## Verdict: REJECT at this price. WATCHLIST below roughly $200

The business clears the Lane A quality bar on returns, balance sheet and brand. It fails on price and on the shape of the setup. Three things kill the buy at 230:

1. The guidance raise that produced the PASS is almost entirely an accounting reclassification plus currency, and it sits on top of a **gross margin guide that was cut by a full point**.
2. The 23% drawdown is a fully retraced post-earnings gap from six weeks ago, not a de-rating. The stock is up 15% from its May close and 18% above where insiders last bought.
3. At 37x forward and a 2.6% FCF yield for a business that has grown revenue 6.3% a year for eight years, the price requires roughly double the FCF growth the company has ever delivered, with no multiple compression. That fails the Lane A margin-of-safety gate outright.

## Item 1: the business. It does clear the quality bar

- One brand doing 97% of sales. WD-40 Multi-Use Product is a category-defining consumer staple with pricing power, sold through hardware, auto and industrial channels in ~176 countries. 714 employees on $620M of revenue.
- **ROIC 23.7%** in the June quarter, and 20-26% every quarter for three years. Above the 20% line the Checklist reserves for the "quality compounder" label.
- **Gross margin 56.6%**, up from 51.4% two years ago, recovering the post-inflation give-back toward the company's own 55%+ target.
- **Fortress balance sheet.** Debt $111M against $59.1M cash, so net debt ~$52M on ~$118M of TTM operating income. Interest coverage above 30x. Net debt/EBITDA well under 0.5x. Nothing here trips the balance-sheet disqualifier.
- **No accounting smell.** Cumulative FY23-25 free cash flow $264.5M against cumulative net income $226.6M, so cash conversion exceeds earnings. The one negative-FCF year (FY22, minus $5.1M) was the supply-chain working-capital build and reversed. Earnings-quality flags are empty.
- One real flag: **receivables grew faster than sales in the June quarter**, $112.4M to $150.1M (+33.5%) against revenue +24.3%. That is DSO moving from about 65 to about 70 days. Worth watching, not disqualifying, and consistent with a promotion-heavy quarter.

Where it fails the Lane A definition rather than the gates: **there is no reinvestment runway.** Capex is $4M a year on $620M of revenue. WD-40 cannot redeploy at 24% because there is almost nothing to deploy into. It pays out 59% of earnings and buys back $10-30M a year against a flat 13.5M share count. This is a cash-return franchise growing with price and geography, not a compounder with a reinvestment engine. That distinction is the whole valuation argument below.

## Item 2: decomposing the guidance raise. This is what changes the verdict

April 9 (Q2) and July 9 (Q3) guidance, on the same constant-currency basis:

| Line | April guide | July guide | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales (constant currency) | $630-655M | $652-667M | midpoint +$17M (+2.6%) |
| Net sales (reported) | not given | $675-690M | FX translation added on top |
| **Gross margin** | **55.5-56.5%** | **54.5-55.5%** | **cut a full point, both ends** |
| Diluted EPS | $5.75-6.15 | $6.05-6.35 | midpoint +$0.25 (+4.2%) |

Now net out what is not operating performance:

- The Americas homecare and cleaning brands were reclassified from held-for-sale back to held-for-use, adding **~$12M of sales, $2.9M of operating income and $0.17 of EPS**. April's release had already flagged roughly $0.20 of EPS upside if the sale failed, so this was a known, pre-announced mechanical add.
- Management describes these brands as non-core **harvest brands expected to decline gradually**. The raise is therefore partly the addition of a shrinking business the company tried and failed to sell.
- Netting that out: the organic constant-currency sales raise is about **$5M, or 0.8%**, and the organic EPS raise is about **$0.08, or 1.3%**. The headline "10-12% reported growth" is 6-9% constant currency plus favourable FX translation, not demand.

And the line that actually matters was cut. Gross margin guidance came down a full point at both ends because **specialty chemical input costs rose by as much as 100% in the quarter, driven by crude oil prices tied to the Iran conflict**, with raw-material sourcing disrupted for third-party manufacturers across Europe, India, the Middle East and Africa. The April guide assumed **crude between $95 and $115 a barrel**. That assumption is the single load-bearing number in the FY27 setup, and this morning's tape has oil at a two-week high and rising on reported Iranian threats to US targets in Europe.

**Against the vault's own rule this cannot be a PASS.** The [[Sourcing Rules]] PASS condition is the GWRE configuration: results delivered with guidance raised or reaffirmed *healthy* while the price fell. A raise built from a reclassification and FX, sitting on a cut to the margin line, is not healthy. On a strict reading of "guidance cut is a KILL, no exceptions" the gross margin line argues for a KILL; on the sales and EPS lines it is a raise. The composite is neither, which is what WATCHLIST is for. Overrule me toward KILL if you want the strict reading; I would not argue hard against it.

## Item 3: the drawdown is a retraced gap, not a dislocation

Gate 8 flagged WDFC at 23% off its 12-month high of $298.90. Here is what that high is:

- 7/9, Q3 reported after close. Stock closed $239.42.
- 7/10, **gapped open at $292.09, touched $298.90 intraday, closed $264.91 on 1.17M shares**, about ten times normal volume.
- Over the next fifteen sessions the entire gap came back. 7/29 low $219.00; 7/31 close $227.14, **below the pre-print price**.

So the market saw this exact "results up, guidance raised" event, bought it violently for one day, re-read it, and sold it back through the starting point. That is close to the opposite of the GWRE setup, where the price fell despite the good news rather than because the market reconsidered it. The screen's drawdown gate measured a one-day spike and called it a dislocation.

The wider picture: 12-month range $175.38 to $298.90, a 70% band on a household-products staple. The stock is **up 15% from its May close of $199.97** and sits mid-range, not dislocated. Dislocation cause tag: this is closer to BREAK than SENT, because the retrace has an identifiable and still-live operating cause in input costs. The [[Sourcing Rules]] baseline notes every winner was SENT and the one BREAK name lagged.

## Item 4: valuation. The gate it actually fails

| Metric | WDFC today | Its own history |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing P/E | 34.7x | 2026 median 35.3, 2025 35.2, 2024 49.9, 2015-2017 28-32 |
| Operating P/E | 26.2x | 2026 median 27.2, 2024 36.1, 2015-2017 19-23 |
| EV/EBIT | 26.6x | 2026 median 27.5, 2015-2017 20-23 |
| Forward P/E | 37.0x FY26E, 35.2x FY27E | consensus EPS $6.22 then $6.53, so +4.9% growth |
| FCF yield | 2.59% | 8-year median around 2.4% |

The 23% drawdown moved this from a bubble multiple to an average one. It is **not cheap on any measure against its own twelve-year history**, and it is expensive against 2015-2018 when the business grew at a similar rate.

Run the price backwards, which the Lane A checklist requires. FY26 free cash flow is tracking about $80M ($51.4M through nine months), roughly flat to down against FY25's $83.8M. At $3.10B that is a 2.6% starting yield. To earn 10% a year for a decade and exit at 30x FCF (still a rich multiple), free cash flow has to compound at roughly 12-13% a year. Demonstrated rates: revenue CAGR 6.3% over FY17-FY25, FCF CAGR 9.0% over FY19-FY25, and FY23 to FY26E is **negative**. The price asks for roughly double the best rate this company has ever sustained, for ten years, with no multiple compression. That is heroic, not conservative, and it is the definition of the margin-of-safety gate failing.

Even the friendly case does not rescue it: the street target of $271.67 is 43.8x FY26E and 41.6x FY27E, so the sell side's own upside requires multiple expansion from an already-full 37x.

One thing to state in the company's favour, because the bear case has been overstated in the press. The April "profit collapse" story compared Q2 FY26 EPS of $1.50 against Q2 FY25's $2.19, and **that prior-year quarter carried a $7.4M tax benefit** (a negative tax line on $22.4M of pre-tax income). Normalised at a 22% rate, Q2 FY25 was about $1.29, so adjusted EPS actually grew ~14%. On the same normalisation FY25 EPS was roughly $5.78 rather than $6.69, which makes the FY26 guide of $6.05-6.35 a genuine 5-10% advance. The earnings are healthier than the headlines said. The price already knows.

## Item 5: the implied Q4, which lands in twelve days

Nine months of FY26 GAAP diluted EPS: $1.28 + $1.50 + $2.24 = **$5.02**. The full-year guide of $6.05-6.35 therefore implies a Q4 of **$1.03 to $1.33 against $1.56 last year**, a decline of 15% to 34%. (Caveat: the guide may be on an adjusted basis, where Q3 was $2.33 rather than $2.24; that makes the implied Q4 worse, not better.)

Normalised, the first three quarters of FY26 grew EPS about 19%. The guide says that stops immediately. Either management is being conservative, or the input-cost cut bites hardest right now, or Q3's promotion-led 24% quarter pulled demand forward. **Fiscal Q4 ends 8/31, twelve days from now, and reports in early October with the first FY27 guide.** That print answers the only question that matters here: what oil at current prices does to FY27 gross margin.

This is the JKHY situation with a longer fuse. On 8/14 you bought JKHY two sessions ahead of the print the thesis rested on, and it was logged as a timing violation. Here the print is seven weeks out and nothing forces the entry: Lane A has no harvest clock.

## What would change the verdict

Three falsifiers, each monitorable, written in advance:

1. **Price.** Below roughly $200 the forward multiple is about 30x and the FCF yield about 3.3%. That is where the October 2025 insider cluster committed and where the Oct-Dec 2025 base sits. Below $175 (the January low) it is genuinely interesting. **Set the watch level at $200.**

3. **Crude.** Management's stated planning band is $95-115. Sustained crude above $115 breaks the margin guide directly. This is the rare falsifier you can watch daily without any company disclosure.
