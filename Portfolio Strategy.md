# Portfolio Strategy

What this note is for: how I build the book. The portfolio-fit and stress-test workflows score against these limits.

> Caps set 2026-06-30 (Tight regime). They exist to restrain my all-in bias (see [[Cognitive Biases]] #4). The wins that taught me concentration works are survivorship; these caps are set a notch tighter than my instinct wants on purpose.

## Concentration caps

Two thresholds per name. **Build-to** is measured at cost: the most I deploy initiating and adding. **Hard-trim ceiling** is measured at market: a winner that grows past it gets trimmed back. The trim at the ceiling is a risk action at a preset line, not a price call, so it does not conflict with [[Sell Discipline]] ("don't sell winners just because they rose").

| Lane | Build-to (at cost) | Hard-trim ceiling (at market) |
|---|---|---|
| Lane A (quality compounder) | 10% | 20% |
| Lane B (insider / special-situation) | 4% | 7% |

- Starter position (thesis not yet confirmed): 2 to 3%, either lane.
- Lane B is smaller by design: a turnaround can go to zero, so it earns less size than a durable compounder.

## Theme cap

A theme is a shared return driver, not a sector label: an end-market demand driver, a key input cost, or a macro factor (rates, the dollar, energy, data-center / AI capex, GLP-1 demand, and the like). Two names can be one theme.

- Soft flag at 20% of the book across one theme: requires written justification in the thesis.
- Hard cap at 30%: do not exceed.
- Every holding is tagged with its driver(s) in [[Holdings]] so the agent can sum exposure. This is the cap that matters most for me; it catches the hidden correlation that turns several names into one bet.

## Minimum diversification

- At least 8 meaningful positions (each above roughly 2% of the book). This makes "all-in on two or three" mechanically impossible, which is the direct cure for my all-in bias.
- If I cannot find 8 ideas that clear the bar, the residual is cash, not a bigger bet on the ideas I have.

## Sizing

- Size to conviction AND to downside, not to excitement. Bigger when the downside is well understood and contained; smaller when the outcome range is wide. Build to target weight in tranches, not all at once.
- On any add, state the downside if I am wrong and what the position does to the book. The agent enforces this per [[Cognitive Biases]] #4.

## New-money rule

- No add that pushes a name past its build-to cap (at cost) or a theme past its hard cap. Above the soft theme flag, the justification gets logged in the thesis or the add does not happen.

## Cash

- A residual of opportunity, not a market call. Hold it when nothing clears the bar.
