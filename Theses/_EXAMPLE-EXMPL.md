# EXMPL: Thesis (SAMPLE)

> This is a fictional example so you can see the structure and test the agent. It is not a real company and not a recommendation. Delete it anytime.

## One-line thesis
A subscription software business with high switching costs that can raise prices and reinvest at high returns for a decade, mispriced because the market extrapolates a temporary growth slowdown.

## How they make money
Annual per-seat SaaS subscriptions; revenue scales with customer headcount and price increases. ~90% gross margins, net revenue retention historically above 115%.

## The three load-bearing claims
1. Switching costs are real and rising (deep workflow integration; retention stays above 110%).
2. Pricing power persists (annual list-price increases stick without elevated churn).
3. The current growth slowdown is cyclical (customer-hiring driven), not structural saturation.

## Why the edge persists (moat)
Switching costs from data and workflow lock-in; secondary network effect across teams. Evidence required: NRR, gross retention, realized price/seat over time.

## Valuation
At entry the price implied ~12% revenue growth fading to GDP with stable margins. Conservative value assumed mid-teens growth for 5 years given the runway; margin of safety ~25%.

## What would make me WRONG (pre-registered disconfirmers)
- Net revenue retention falls below 105% for two consecutive quarters (lock-in breaking).
- A credible competitor wins two or more marquee accounts on price (pricing power cracking).
- Management blames "macro" for a third straight quarter while seat growth and NRR both deteriorate (structural, not cyclical).

## Monitoring triggers
- Each earnings report: NRR, gross retention, realized price/seat, net new logos.
- Any large customer loss disclosed in an 8-K.
